Why train, and what does the model read?

On teaching twelve blocks one corpus, and the shape of what they read.

The playground, step 4, training · the training trail

A Thread trains so it can recall its corpus. Its head, twelve blocks of its own, learns to predict its records word for word in front of the commons' frozen tail, and the states they pass through become the keys that find the right place in that text later.

This is memorisation on purpose. A Thread is a keeper of receipts, not a generaliser. The playground's Thread learned its 18 records in 80 steps.

What the model reads

Training reads one long stream: an end-of-text marker, the first record, another marker, the next record, and so on to the last. The playground's stream is 645 tokens. A grid of windows is laid over it, shifted a little every epoch, and a batch of windows is one step.

Each marker does two jobs. The model's state resets there, so every record is read from a blank state, exactly as the index will read it. And the loss is masked there, because a record's first word can't be predicted from the record before it.

The real run

Here is the run, as the Thread recorded it. The loss starts at 4.5 nats a token, the base model's surprise at records it has never seen, and falls as the twelve blocks learn them.

Every 40 steps the whole corpus is scored, one record at a time. The run stops at the first score that clears the bar: 97% of positions memorised, meaning the right next token gets at least 90.5% of the probability. The budget was 300 steps. The run needed 80.

Memorised counts positions the model can repeat, and nothing more. Braid treats it as a lower bound on what a Thread holds: the bench meant to catch memorised text that the index cannot cite failed on 2026-10-15.

For model engineers: the recipe, and what trains
OptimizerAdamW, β (0.9, 0.95), ε 1e-8, weight decay 0.01, gradient clip 1.0
Schedulewarmup over 5% of the planned steps from 1% of the peak, then cosine from 5e-4 to 1e-4
Budget1,000 steps for a new Thread, 500 continuing; the playground asks for 300 at windows of 128
Evaluationevery 40 steps; learned at 97% memorised, loss under 0.1 nat, and 90% of facts when facts are located
Gradientsonly the head's blocks 0–11 carry them; backprop runs through all 24, the commons' tail included, which never changes

No facts are located in an app's records, so the facts half of “learned” was vacuous for the harbour Thread. The window grid's offset and order come from a seeded generator; the figure above runs a port of it. thread sync trains in windows of 512, and a corpus shorter than 513 tokens cannot fill one.

Measured on 2026-10-15 on an M4 Max, release build, chunk 256: a step at 8 × 512 takes 0.81 s and peaks at 22.9 GB; at 8 × 1,024, 1.86 s and 44.6 GB. Chunk 128 is faster and leaner at every shape; the default stays 256 until the owner changes it.

A Thread learns its corpus by heart, read the way it will be cited: one record at a time, from a blank state.
