What is AII, and how does an app join it?

On the index every app on a Mac shares, and the lane each one gets.

The playground, step 1 · the playground

The Ambient Intelligence Index is one index per Mac. It lives in ~/.rao, and every app on the machine shares it: notes, code, images, whatever an app files. Nothing has to leave the computer.

Two servers do the work. The Thread service is the index itself: it stores what apps file, embeds it and searches it. Sewn answers questions grounded in what the index holds. Seam is the wire between them.

Each app gets a lane. A lane is the app's share of the index, opened with a secret only that app holds. An app reads and writes its own lane and nothing else, and the secret decides which lane a call is in, whatever the call says.

Braid has no lane of its own. To learn from an app's records it presents that app's secret and reads the lane the app filed into. A Braid Thread is one lane, read with its secret.

Joining

An app becomes a lane by joining. The playground declares a name, braidplayground, and a display name, and that is the whole declaration.

The first join of a new name may make the servers restart once. After that, every run adopts them as they are.

Letting go is the one thing to get right. Joining takes a lease on the servers, and the last app to let go stops them. The playground drops its own hold and never calls leave(), which would stop the Mac's servers for every app on it.

Explore: one store, many lanes

Every app's records sit in the same three files. The lane is a set of entries in one registry, and every read is cut to the caller's lane before anything is scanned.

For model engineers: lanes, owners, and where Braid meets AII

AIIInstance is an actor. A lane name is 2 to 32 lowercase letters and digits, starting with a letter, and never aii. Registering writes a 64-hex secret to secrets/<app> and an entry to aii/lanes.json; the lane id is derived from a device key, so a lane cannot be named by a client.

Within a lane, records belong to an owner: local-<app> for an app with no account, aii-local for the Rao apps. Groups are <app>-<group> on the wire. The stack identity, aii, reads every lane and writes none.

Braid pins AII at 691b40e, where any registered app is a lane. Its corpus source for an app's records is .app("home/braidplayground"), written app:home/braidplayground: home is this Mac's ~/.rao, and any other name is a demo AII that braid aii create provisions under aii/, on ports 47181/47190 and up.

One index per Mac, one lane per app, and a lane opens only with its own secret.
