Why does a Thread need the whole corpus?

On the difference between finding a few passages and reading everything.

The playground, step 4, the snapshot · the training trail

Search answers a question with a view. It picks the documents the index's graph lets through, takes the best three passages of each, and ranks them by distance. That is the right shape for an answer, and the wrong shape for learning.

A Thread has to read every record whole, in a fixed order, from its first token. Every position will be keyed, cited and re-read later, so nothing can be missing, and the order can't change between runs.

So AII has a second door. ExportCorpus returns every record an owner filed, whole, page by page, in id order. The service's own definition says why.

The snapshot

Braid exports the app's records, keeps their text, sorts them by id, and hashes the result. The hash is the receipt for what was read. Change one character of one record and it changes, and the Thread's next version says so.

Every token has an address

Then each partition is cut into tokens on its own, so any offset can be found again from that partition's text alone. A document becomes an end-of-text marker, its partitions with a paragraph break between them, and another marker. Every token in it gets an address: its partition's row, and its offset.

That address is what a citation points at. The harbour answer's first word is cited to row 5, offset 19.

Explore: why the search missed, and what the export reads

A search runs through the graph before it ranks anything. If what a query names leads nowhere in the caller's lane, nothing is scanned at all.

The whole storage path, on one page

For model engineers: the export, the hash, the addressing

Braid's .app source asks for every record under the lane's owner, every group that owner holds, ids under braidplayground/, 25 records a page with partition ids and URLs. It keeps each partition's text, index, text hash, URL and Thread partition id; it drops the envelope and any vectors. Braid never searches.

Tokenizing keeps each partition separate. Break tokens are addressed to the preceding partition at offsets at or past its token count, so a chain can run across them and nothing is ever cited to one. A rerun with unchanged records plans “up to date” and trains nothing.

Search finds a passage. A Thread needs the text, all of it, in order, under a hash.
