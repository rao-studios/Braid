Board 1 · A Thread's blocks: a head of twelve, on the commons' tail

board 1 · Braid v1 · drawn 2026-10-19, after the boards of 2026-10-15 · mamba2-130m-hf · no attention, no KV cache

Every strand in the braid runs the same 24 Mamba2 blocks. A Thread owns the first twelve, its head, trained on its own corpus. The last twelve, the final norm and the embedding are the commons' tail: loaded once when the commons is built and run by every Thread behind its own head, by reference. There is no attention and no KV cache. Each block keeps a small recurrent state, so everything a node knows about a conversation is a fixed 19,390,464 bytes, however long it runs. The residual stream after the head, paired with the final state, is the key into the Thread's provenance index, and any key can be recomputed from a saved state.

Board 2 · The braid: the commons and every Thread under one reed

board 2 · Braid v1 · drawn 2026-10-19, after the boards of 2026-10-15 · blocks: board 1 · in detail: board 3

The braid is the commons, the pack's base model run whole, and every attached Thread, under one reed. At every token each node advances its own state and sends two things: its normed state h and its 16 nearest positions in its own corpus. The braid turns each into a distribution, weighs them by a memory of how much better than the commons each Thread has predicted, and picks the token. Every strand's share of that token is exact, and each Thread's part is cited to its own text. Every number goes into a session log that braid verify replays. Threads can join or leave in the middle of an answer.
