What happens at each token?

On what the braid does between one word and the next.

The playground, step 6 · the playground

Nothing is carried over from the last question. When a prompt arrives, every strand starts from a blank state, reads the prompt from its first token, and looks up its own index at every position along the way. The commons reads it too. Then the braid writes the answer, one token at a time.

At each token, every strand makes two guesses. Its reed guesses the next token from what it has read. Its index guesses from the sixteen places in its own corpus that look most like this moment. A Thread blends the two half and half; the commons has no index, so it guesses with its reed alone.

Then the gate weighs the strands, the braid takes the likeliest token, and every strand's share of that token is written down. The shares add up to exactly one. That line of the log is the receipt.

One token, split exactly

Take a single token and the arithmetic is short. Each strand's probability for the token is weighted by the gate. The weighted probabilities add up to the token's probability. Each strand's share is its part of that sum.

Nothing is estimated after the fact. The split is how the token was chosen, so the shares can't disagree with the answer, and they always add up to one.

The gate learns only while the prompt is read

The gate is a memory of who has been predicting well. It starts with nearly all its trust on the commons, because the base model knows ordinary language. Every prompt token is a small test: a Thread that predicted it better than the commons gains trust, and one that predicted it worse gives some back.

No single token can move the gate by more than ten to one. That clamp is why one lucky word can't hand a source the answer. And once the answer starts, the memory freezes, so a Thread can't vote for itself with its own words.

Sources can come and go mid-answer

A Thread can join while an answer is being written. It reads the session so far in one pass, catches up, and takes its share of the Threads' trust at the next token boundary. A Thread that leaves hands its trust back the same way.

The log records both moves, and every step is read through the strands it was written with. So a citation never lands on the wrong source after a change, and the receipt still adds up.

Questions go through a stem

A question is harder to complete than a sentence. Braid asks the commons to rewrite it the way the corpus would say it, up to a blank. Each Thread puts the sentence it holds about the subject in front. The braid completes the stem and stops at the end of the sentence.

The 130m commons is a weak rewriter. It writes the corpus's own stem for 7% of questions, which is why the playground finishes a sentence instead of asking one.

When an answer does come out exact, it cites the right place. In the question bench, every one of 14 exact answers cited the fact's own partition.

For model engineers: one token, call by call

A generation is Cluster.generate. It opens every strand on eos ‖ prompt with one forward pass from a zero state, scores every prompt position with all strands open while the gate observes, then loops: apply pending attaches and detaches, run the reed once per strand, build each strand's Expert, step its trace tracker, open the strands at or above the floor, mix, choose, record one tok line, push the module, advance every node.

The gate itself, whole. Credibility is a running average of each strand's gains; the weight on every ratio is the best Thread credibility; a strand that lost gives up a tenth of its memory per unit of loss, spread evenly over the others.

The mixture is written as (1 − λ̄)·Σ reedW·p_lm + λ̄·Σ knnW·p_knn, which is the same sum as Σ w′·p_t. The choice is the argmax, except at a near tie within one part in a million, where the token whose cited text is oldest wins, then the lower id. Every number that chooses a token or sets a share is a double on the CPU.

Citations are derived from the log, never stored. A hit matches when its value is the chosen token; matching hits group by partition, and a span needs a matching hit at rank three or better for at least three tokens walking one partition position by position. Break positions are never cited. Confidence is (simTerm(cos)·support·e^−loss)^⅓: evidence of memorisation and retrieval support, not proof of influence.

Credit is a second view: a whole-word form token goes to the commons, a content token to each strand by its share, weighted by bits = −log₂ p_commons(y). Where two Threads hold the same fact, the one whose chain the sentence followed takes the credit, by a margin of two tokens; within the margin they split it. Weight never decides.

A log costs about 2 KB per Thread per token, measured on 2026-10-15, against the plan's target of 2.5 KB at three Threads. It is self-contained, so verify needs no index.

Nothing here is inferred afterwards. Every share was written down when its token was chosen.
